## Notice, contesting automated decisions, due process (replaces PDF [10] and [11])

7. B. Bayamlıoğlu, "The right to contest automated decisions under the General Data Protection Regulation: Beyond the so-called 'right to explanation'," *Regulation & Governance*, 2022. DOI 10.1111/rego.12391. Status: search result.
8. Lyons et al., "What's the Appeal? Perceptions of Review Processes for Algorithmic Decisions," *CHI 2022*. DOI 10.1145/3491102.3517606. Status: search result. Finding to state as a limit: appeal conditions did not improve fairness perceptions, and appealing a computer's decision to another computer was reported as frustrating.
9. Yurrita et al., "Disentangling Fairness Perceptions in Algorithmic Decision-Making: the Effects of Explanations, Human Oversight, and Contestability," *CHI 2023*. Status: search result; no link found.
10. J. Cai, A. Patel, A. Naderi and D. Y. Wohn, "Content Moderation Justice and Fairness on Social Media: Comparisons Across Different Contexts and Platforms," *CHI LBW 2024*, arXiv:2403.06034. https://arxiv.org/pdf/2403.06034. Status: checked.
11. C. Drolsbach and N. Pröllochs, "Content Moderation on Social Media in the EU: Insights From the DSA Transparency Database," arXiv:2312.04431, 2023. https://arxiv.org/pdf/2312.04431. Status: checked.
12. Regulation (EU) 2022/2065 (Digital Services Act), Articles 16, 17 and 20 (notice and action, statement of reasons, internal complaint handling). Status: from memory and search results. Check Article 20 for the wording on complaint decisions and automated means before citing it; if it requires qualified staff, it argues against removing the human step.
